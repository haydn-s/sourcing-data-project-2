# Study Videos and Quiz Questions

Each participant watches both videos: one with a fidget toy and one without. The videos are
matched as closely as possible so that differences in quiz scores come from the fidget toy, not
from one video being easier.

## Selected Videos

Both are TED-Ed lessons about animal behavior. They are almost the same length, use the same
animated narration style, and don't require any science background.

## Counterbalancing

Every participant does both conditions, but the order of the videos and conditions rotates across
four groups. This way each video is watched with and without the fidget toy equally often, so any
difference in video difficulty (or a warm-up/tiredness effect from going first or second) cancels
out.

| Group | First video | First condition | Second video | Second condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A | Fidget toy | B | No fidget toy |
| 2 | A | No fidget toy | B | Fidget toy |
| 3 | B | Fidget toy | A | No fidget toy |
| 4 | B | No fidget toy | A | Fidget toy |

Assign participants to groups in rotation (1, 2, 3, 4, 1, 2, …) as they arrive. To keep the
groups the same size, aim for a total that is a multiple of 4 (for example, 36 instead of 34).

## Running a Session

Total time is about 15–20 minutes per participant.

1. Give the participant an ID (P01, P02, …) and note their group number.
2. Play the first video full screen on the study laptop at a fixed volume. Hand over the fidget
   toy only if their group starts with it.
3. Right after the video, the participant takes the quiz for that video.
4. Take a 2-minute break. Swap the fidget toy (take it back, or hand it over).
5. Play the second video in the other condition.
6. The participant takes the quiz for the second video.

Use the same laptop, volume and setup for every session. Check beforehand that no ads play
before the videos.

## Quiz Questions

To be added once the number of questions is decided. The quiz form reads its questions from
[`../questions.js`](../questions.js), which has one question set per video; keep that file and the
tables below in step. Guidelines:

- Only ask about facts that are stated in the video, not general knowledge.
- Spread the questions across the whole video (beginning, middle and end).
- Use the same number of questions for both videos, with a similar mix of easy and harder ones.
- Each question has four choices (A–D) with one correct answer, to match the quiz form.
- Avoid questions so easy that everyone gets a perfect score, or there will be no room to see a
  difference between conditions.
